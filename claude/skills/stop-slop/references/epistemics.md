# Epistemics: What the Text Is Allowed to Claim

Vocabulary and structure fixes (see phrases.md, structures.md) don't reach
the deepest tell: AI text is evenly confident and fully resolved
everywhere, because it's assembled rather than reported. Fixing that means
controlling what gets claimed, not just how it's phrased.

## Never invent specifics

Numbers, quotes, names, dates, and thresholds must come from the source
material or the user. A specific that sounds textured but isn't backed by
anything is worse than a vague statement, because it reads as authoritative.

Before writing a specific, know which bucket it's in:

- **Data**: given directly by the source or the user. State it plainly.
- **Computed claim**: derived from given data. Name the assumption it rests
  on. "At the current rate (3 issues/week), the backlog clears in 4 months"
  — not "the backlog will clear soon."
- **Judgment call**: an opinion, not a fact. Mark it as one. "I'd cut the
  third option" — not "the third option should be cut."
- **Acknowledged gap**: something not known. Say so instead of papering
  over it with a plausible-sounding guess.

If a specific doesn't fit one of these, it's invented. Cut it or replace it
with the vague-but-honest version.

## Allow uneven confidence

Real writing is confident where the writer has grounds to be and hedged
where they don't. Hedges like "probably" or "I think" should mark an actual
epistemic state, not get sprinkled everywhere as reflexive safety, and
shouldn't vanish everywhere either for false authority. Uniform confidence
across an entire piece is itself a tell.

## Don't chew the conclusion

AI text over-explains its own ending: restating the argument, spelling out
the implication, adding a lesson. Human writing ends on the last necessary
thought and stops. If the piece has made its point, the last sentence should
not be a summary of the points already made.

## Name assumptions and judgment calls explicitly

Don't launder an assumption into a stated fact. "Assuming the current team
size holds" is one clause; leaving it out and stating the conclusion as fact
is a different, false claim.

## Reread checklist

Before delivering text, check:

- Any specific number, quote, or name that isn't traceable to a source?
- Any claim stated as fact that's actually a computed estimate or a
  judgment call?
- Is confidence uniform across the piece, or does it track what's actually
  known?
- Does the ending restate/summarize instead of just stopping?
- Any gap in the material papered over instead of named?
